How to win social deduction games: tips for Werewolf, Avalon and more

Werewolf, Avalon, Mafia or Cheese Thief: they all come down to watching, talking and voting. Practical tips for both the good and the evil side.

Good side: find the truth with logic

  • Track what everyone says and how they vote. Contradictions and sudden vote changes are key clues.
  • Watch the very quiet and the very eager: evil players often avoid taking a side, or rush to lead.
  • Look at relationships, not just individuals. Who keeps defending each other? Who never suspects each other?
  • Information roles (Seer, Merlin, Detective) must time their reveal: too early and they are targeted, too late and the information is wasted.

Evil side: blend in

  • Think like a good player: who would they suspect right now, and how would they vote? Do the same.
  • Do not stay too close to your teammates. If needed, throw one under the bus to earn trust.
  • Keep your lies simple and consistent. The more complex the story, the easier it breaks.
  • In mission games like Avalon or The Resistance, you do not have to fail every mission. Letting one pass can protect your cover.

How to speak

  • Start with your conclusion, then the reason. "I suspect player 3 because…" beats a long analysis.
  • Offer things others can check, like "If I were evil, I would not have voted that way last night."
  • When suspected, stay calm and point out the gaps in the reasoning instead of simply denying it.

How to vote

  • Before voting, ask yourself: how bad is it if I am wrong?
  • Pointing or voting at the same time avoids bandwagons. Board Game MC counts down "three, two, one".
  • The vote itself is a clue: who saved whom, and who voted against what they said?

Common mistakes

  • Random voting on day one. With no information, listen to another round first.
  • Trusting only your gut. Gut feelings help, but back them with logic and the vote record.
  • Taking it personally. Lying is part of the rules; stay friends after the game.
